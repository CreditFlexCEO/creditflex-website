Class 2: Understanding Your Credit Score

Beginner level • Approximately 15 minutes • Free educational course

Understand what credit scores measure, why different scores may appear, and what habits can help over time.

Lesson 1: What is a credit score?

A credit score is a number produced by a scoring model using information in a credit report. Lenders may use scores as one factor in lending decisions. A score is not the same thing as your credit report, and no score guarantees loan approval.

Many commonly used FICO and VantageScore models use a range of 300 to 850, but some scoring models use other ranges.

Lesson 2: Why you may have different scores

You do not have only one credit score. Different scoring companies, model versions, dates, and credit-bureau data can produce different numbers. The score displayed by a monitoring app may not be the exact score a lender uses.

Lesson 3: What influences your score?

  • Payment history: On-time payments matter. Late payments may hurt your score.
  • Credit utilization: Using a smaller portion of available revolving credit is generally better than carrying high card balances.
  • Length of credit history: Older accounts can help establish a longer history.
  • Credit mix: Experience with different types of credit may be considered; do not borrow solely to create a mix.
  • New credit: Multiple new applications and certain hard inquiries may affect scores.

Exact weights depend on the scoring model and the information in your file.

Lesson 4: Practical ways to build credit

  • Pay every bill by its due date; set reminders or automatic payments when affordable.
  • Review credit reports for inaccuracies and dispute errors with evidence.
  • Try to keep credit card balances low relative to limits.
  • Avoid opening unnecessary accounts simply to chase a score.
  • Allow time for positive history to develop; there is no guaranteed quick fix.

Checking your own credit report is a soft inquiry and does not lower your credit score. Carrying a balance or paying interest is not required to build a good credit score.

Lesson 5: Common myths

Myth: Everyone has the same score across every app and lender. Fact: Models and bureau data differ.

Myth: Checking your own report hurts your score. Fact: Your own checks do not lower it.

Myth: Paying interest on a credit card improves your score. Fact: You do not need to pay interest to build credit.

Learn more at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.
