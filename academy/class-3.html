Class 3: Building and Improving Your Credit

Beginner level • Approximately 15 minutes • Free educational course

Learn practical credit-building habits without promises of instant score increases.

Lesson 1: Pay on time

Payment history is important in many scoring models. Make a list of due dates and set reminders. If you can safely afford automatic payments, consider using them, but monitor your account to avoid overdraft fees. If you cannot make a payment, contact the lender promptly to ask about hardship options. An arrangement does not automatically prevent negative reporting.

Lesson 2: Manage credit card utilization

Credit utilization compares a revolving account's reported balance with its credit limit. For example, a $300 reported balance on a $1,000 limit is 30% utilization. Lower utilization is generally better, but there is no universal threshold that guarantees a particular score. Paying balances down can help; carrying interest-bearing debt is not required.

Lesson 3: Start with limited credit history

A secured credit card may help establish payment history if the issuer reports to credit bureaus. Secured cards typically require a refundable deposit and may charge fees or interest. Compare terms and verify reporting before applying. Some credit-builder loans may also report payments, but they can have fees and should be evaluated carefully. Do not borrow solely to improve a score.

Lesson 4: Avoid common setbacks

  • Do not apply for multiple accounts without a reason.
  • Review the annual percentage rate, fees, and repayment terms before accepting credit.
  • Do not ignore collection notices or possible identity theft.
  • Check your credit reports for inaccuracies; dispute specific errors with supporting evidence.
  • Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed credit-score increases or removal of accurate information.

Lesson 5: Build a realistic plan

Choose one manageable step for the next month: set up payment reminders, review your reports, or create a plan to reduce revolving balances. Check progress periodically. Credit improvement depends on your individual report and scoring model; it may take time and results vary.
