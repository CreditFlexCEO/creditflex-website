Lesson 1 of 5

Meet Your Credit Report

A credit report is a record of how you have used credit. It may show loans, credit cards, payments, collections, and who has checked your credit.

A credit report is not a credit score. A score is a number calculated from information in a report.

Three major nationwide credit reporting companies are Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Their information can differ. You can request free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com.

Your first job: check that the accounts and details actually belong to you.

Lesson 3 of 5

Find the Mistake

Help Angela investigate one issue at a time. Read each clue, choose a next step, and see why it matters.

An unfamiliar item deserves investigation, but it is not automatically proof of fraud or a reporting error.

Lesson 4 of 5

Help Angela Fix It

Angela starts by comparing dates and account details. If she finds an error, she saves copies of relevant statements, receipts, and letters.

  1. Identify the exact mistake. Write down which account and which detail are wrong.
  2. Collect evidence. Keep copies, not the only originals.
  3. Dispute inaccurate or incomplete information. Contact the credit reporting company and, when appropriate, the business that provided the information.
  4. Review the result. The information may be corrected, deleted, or verified as accurate. If unresolved, review the explanation and consider next steps.

Investigations generally take about 30 days, with some circumstances allowing up to 45 days. Accurate negative information cannot simply be erased because it is unwanted. Nobody can guarantee a deletion or score increase.

For identity theft concerns, visit IdentityTheft.gov. For dispute guidance, visit the CFPB.

Practice dispute note: “My store card shows a late payment for August. My attached payment confirmation shows the payment was made on time. Please investigate and correct the payment history if inaccurate.”
