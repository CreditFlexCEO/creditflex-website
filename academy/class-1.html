First, what exactly is a credit report?

S
Sunny explains

“Think of a credit report as a record of certain credit accounts and how they've been handled. It may show your loans, credit cards, balances, payment history, collections, and who has checked your credit.

A credit score is different. It's a number calculated from credit information. Today we're learning to understand the information in the report itself, not promising anyone a particular score.”

Credit report

Account details, balances, history, inquiries

Credit score

A number calculated from credit information

Where to get yours: The official federally authorized site for free reports from the nationwide credit reporting companies is AnnualCreditReport.com. The three major nationwide companies are Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Lesson 4 · Guided investigation

What should Angela do when something looks wrong?

S
Sunny teaches

“Now Angela finds a store-card payment marked 30 days late. She believes she paid on time. We won't assume the report is wrong, and we won't assume it's right. We'll check her payment confirmation and the due date first.

If the information is inaccurate, Angela can dispute it with the credit reporting company and, as appropriate, the company that provided the information. She should explain exactly what's wrong and include copies of relevant evidence, not originals.”
