Class 1: Understanding Your Credit Report

Beginner level • Approximately 15 minutes • Free educational course

Learn how to read your credit report, spot possible mistakes, and take the right next steps.

Lesson 1: What is a credit report?

A credit report is a record of information about your borrowing and repayment history. It may show credit cards, loans, balances, payment history, collections, and inquiries. A credit report is not the same as a credit score. A score is calculated using information from credit reports.

Lesson 2: The three credit bureaus

Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion are the three major nationwide credit reporting companies. Their reports may differ because not every lender reports to every bureau.

You can request free reports through AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized website.

Lesson 3: How to read your report

  • Personal information: Check your name and addresses.
  • Accounts: Check the lender, balance, account status, and dates.
  • Payment history: Review whether reported late payments are accurate.
  • Collections: Check that any listed debt belongs to you and the details are correct.
  • Inquiries: Review who accessed your report and why.

A negative item is not necessarily an error simply because it lowers a score.

Lesson 4: Spotting and disputing errors

Look for accounts that are not yours, incorrect balances, payments incorrectly marked late, and duplicate reporting that inaccurately represents the same debt. Save documents that support your claim.

Dispute inaccurate or incomplete information with the credit bureau and, when appropriate, the company that supplied the information. Explain the specific error and provide supporting documents. Credit reporting companies generally investigate disputes within 30 days, though some investigations can take 45 days.

Accurate negative information generally cannot be removed just because you dispute it. CreditFlex does not guarantee deletions or score increases.
